### 1. The starting surface

![A bare concrete roof with solar water heaters](step-01.jpg)

The roof was bare concrete, with solar water heaters and a lot of pipework that makes any job harder.

### 2. The narrow passages

![A narrow passage between a wall and the parapet](step-02.jpg)

There are very narrow passages around the rooftop room. They need insulating just as carefully as the open roof.

### 3. Bitumen primer

![The walkway by the railings coated with black primer](step-03.jpg)

First a bitumen primer goes on everywhere, including the foot of the walls.

### 4. Around the solar heaters

![Primer under and around the solar water heaters](step-04.jpg)

The primer reaches under the solar heaters and between the pipes, so no spot is left uncovered.

### 5. Spraying

![Spraying polyurethane foam in a protective suit and mask](step-05.jpg)

Polyurethane foam is sprayed over the whole surface and around the bases. It bonds to the primer and forms one layer with no joints.

### 6. The solar heater bases

![Foam around the bases of the solar heaters](step-06.jpg)

The foam wraps the solar heater bases and pipes, where leaks usually start.

### 7. The result

![The walkway by the railings covered in foam](step-07.jpg)

The roof is now insulated and watertight from end to end, even in the hardest spots.
