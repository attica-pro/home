### 1. The roof before

![The roof with its old dark membrane and railings](step-01.jpg)

The roof was covered in an old bitumen membrane full of patches. Once a membrane gets to this point, one more patch won't fix the problem.

### 2. Stripping

![Pieces of old membrane torn up on the roof](step-02.jpg)

The membrane is cut and stripped off piece by piece.

### 3. Membrane that had come away

![The old membrane rolling up as it comes off the roof](step-03.jpg)

In many places the membrane rolled up like a carpet, because it had already come unstuck. Water could run freely underneath it.

### 4. A wrinkled surface

![Close-up of the old membrane, wrinkled and worn](step-04.jpg)

Sun and age had wrinkled and cracked its surface, so it no longer protected the roof.

### 5. A clean base

![The roof without the membrane, with the last pieces being cleared](step-05.jpg)

The leftovers are cleared and the surface is cleaned, so the new material bonds straight to the slab.

### 6. White waterproofing

![A tin and a roller by the low wall under the railings](step-06.jpg)

The white waterproofing is spread over the roof and up the low wall under the railings, around each of their posts.

### 7. The joints

![The low wall and its corner with the floor coated white](step-07.jpg)

The corner between the floor and the low wall gets extra care, because that's usually where the first cracks open.

### 8. The result

![The white roof with its railings and satellite dishes](step-08.jpg)

A clean white surface, bonded across its whole area, with no joints or patches.
