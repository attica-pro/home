### 1. Torn membranes

![Torn and lifted membranes on the roof](step-01.jpg)

The joints of the old membranes had opened and pieces had lifted. Water was getting in underneath the insulation there.

### 2. Stripping

![Stripping the old membranes](step-02.jpg)

A membrane that has come unstuck can't be fixed with patches. It has to come off completely.

### 3. Cleaning and priming

![The roof without the old membranes](step-03.jpg)

With the membranes gone, the real state of the slab shows. It is cleaned and primed so the foam bonds well.

### 4. Spray polyurethane foam

![The roof covered in cream polyurethane foam](step-04.jpg)

Spray polyurethane foam goes over the whole surface and up the parapets. It is one layer with no joints, so there are no seams left to open up.

### 5. A liquid membrane on top

![The white membrane around an air conditioning stand](step-05.jpg)

A white liquid polyurethane membrane (Hyperdesmo) goes over the foam, waterproofing it and protecting it from the sun. It wraps the air-conditioner stands and chimneys, the most vulnerable spots, on every side.

### 6. The result

![The finished roof](step-06.jpg)

One continuous white roof that reflects the sun and has no more places for water to get through.
