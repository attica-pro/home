### 1. Where we started

![A terrace with stained old slabs](step-01.jpg)

The slabs were stained and worn with age, and the joints between them are always a likely place for leaks.

### 2. Grinding

![An angle grinder on the terrace, next to the parapet](step-02.jpg)

The grinder removes hard residue and uneven spots, especially near the parapets.

### 3. A clean surface

![The cleaned terrace, with a metal post](step-03.jpg)

The surface is cleared of dust and loose material so the coating bonds well.

### 4. Post bases

![The base of a metal post sealed with white material](step-04.jpg)

Each post set into the slab first gets a ring of white sealant, because cracks open easily there.

### 5. The railings

![A railing base sealed on the parapet](step-05.jpg)

The same goes for the railing bases on top of the parapet.

### 6. Primer

![The terrace glossy after priming](step-06.jpg)

Primer goes over the whole surface. It soaks into the pores of the slabs and helps the waterproofing stick.

### 7. White waterproofing

![White waterproofing over the primer, with the tin and roller](step-07.jpg)

The white waterproofing is rolled on over the primer and taken up the parapets.

### 8. The result

![The white terrace with the solar water heater](step-08.jpg)

One continuous white surface with no joints. It reflects the sun and keeps the floor below cooler.
