Every piece of wood wears in its own way, but the order of the work is much the same. Let's follow it on a set of old painted shutters that were sanded, primed and repainted instead of being replaced.

## 1. Taking them down and protecting the area

On this job the leaves were taken out of their frames and worked on standing up or laid flat, with cardboard protecting the floor. With a leaf out of its frame, the sanding and painting reach every side, including the bottom ends, which you don't see but which take the most water.

## 2. Scraping and sanding

Old paint that isn't holding comes off with a scraper, and then the wood is sanded with a power sander. The aim is to reach sound wood and not leave ridges of old paint that would show under the new coat. On the louvres and mouldings, where the sander doesn't fit, it usually takes sandpaper by hand.

![A shutter leaf being sanded, with the sander and scraper resting on it](wooden-shutters-restoration/step-02.jpg)

## 3. Repairs

Sanding reveals what the wood has been hiding. Small holes and cracks are filled with wood filler and sanded again so they don't show under the paint. Joints that have opened up need tightening. Rotten spots need a different approach: the damaged wood is cut out and new wood is fitted, or, if the damage is extensive, the leaf is replaced. Which of the two applies is decided at that point, on the wood itself.

## 4. Primer

Once the wood is clean and dry, the primer goes on. It seals the grain so the paint isn't soaked up unevenly, and gives a firm base for the coats that follow. Primer doesn't come out even, because the wood doesn't absorb it the same way everywhere. That's why the coats of paint come next.

![The shutters primed, with the primer taken up unevenly](wooden-shutters-restoration/step-03.jpg)

## 5. Final coats

Then come the coats of paint or varnish, with a light sanding in between where needed. The bottom ends and the edges need the same care as the front face, because that's where water gets in first. Hardware such as hinges and handles is either protected or painted too. Once everything is properly dry, the leaves go back in place.

In the end, the same shutters that had been peeling had a clean, even colour again.
